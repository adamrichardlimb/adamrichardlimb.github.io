Delayed Signals

On outdated status-signalling.
Author Adam Limb

One of the perks of living in the countryside is that there are a lot of good walks all around you. One of the downsides of living in the countryside is that there's not much to do but walk. Fortunately, I am engaged to the most (objectively) intelligent and beautiful woman in the world and so a lot of these walks get me thinking about interesting topics to write about.

On one of these walks we discussed this sketch from That Mitchell and Webb Look which aired in 2010. The fictional supermarket known as 'Didldidi' parodies Lidl down to the colour scheme and font. Today, the downmarket supermarket has ceased to be a figure of fun and instead become a place where even Hattie, governer of her daughters primary and Parkrun enthusiast goes to pick up her bits.

In spite of this, the occassional glimmer of noughties mockery of 'Tesco Value', of own-brand beans over the superior Heinz still lingers in those for whom the noughties were formative, or those for whom the signal is a means to alleviate their own insecurity.

In this regard, social signals differ from their physical counterparts. The delayed signal is sent out with the hope that it would be received in the context of its time, and is instead an indicator of a personal failure to either keep up with the current social order, or an insecurity about the impending social order.
