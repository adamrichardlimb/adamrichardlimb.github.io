Delayed Signals

On outdated status-signalling.
Author Adam Limb

One of the perks of living in the countryside is that there are a lot of good walks all around you. One of the downsides of living in the countryside is that there’s not much to do but walk. Fortunately, I am engaged to the most (objectively) intelligent and beautiful woman in the world and so a lot of these walks get me thinking about interesting topics to write about.

On one of these walks we discussed this sketch from That Mitchell and Webb Look which aired in 2010. The fictional supermarket known as ‘Didldidi’ parodies Lidl down to the colour scheme and font. Today, the downmarket supermarket has ceased to be a figure of fun and instead become a place where even Hattie, governer of her daughters primary and Parkrun enthusiast goes to pick up her bits.

In spite of this, the occassional glimmer of noughties mockery of ‘Tesco Value’, of own-brand beans over the superior Heinz still lingers in those for whom the noughties were formative, or those for whom the signal is a means to alleviate their own insecurity.

In this regard, social signals differ from their physical counterparts. The delayed signal is sent out with the hope that it would be received in the context of its time, and is instead an indicator of a personal failure to either keep up with the current social order, or an insecurity about the impending social order. Despite the world firmly moving on from chavs, austerity, and ‘things can only get better’, for those whose political lives were at their xenith in the noughties, all of the modern malaise is best explained by nasty Tories cutting council budgets as opposed to the huge adult social care budgets those councils require.

Delayed signals also waste your time. Look across any Waterstones and you’ll find a cornicopia of drek made possible by the cheap cost of paper, the advancements made in supply chains, and a more globalised economy. Still, reading books is still considered a sign of erudition. This signal is so delayed that women actively broadcast their literary porn habits as though they were plucky Oxbridge graduates consuming the canon. None of this is intended to descend into a Kazynskian/quasi-Marxist critique of “““modernity”““. Self-publishing via Amazon is a boon to humanity, whether economically through giving women their fix of minotaurs, centaurs, and other fairytale rapists, or through allowing thinkers who would otherwise be forgotten to share their works with the world. Instead, this article is a warning against accepting social signifiers as they are. Today, every half-baked right wing grifter has a book which acts as a summary of their twitter feed since their last book, which was largely the same thing.

Thankfully, the very technological changes that bring about a delay in social signals also expedite the recognition of their outdated nature. Rather than lament ChatGPT and other LLM’s flooding bookstores with artificially generated crap, we are forced to recognise that artificially generated crap is only able to be competitive with humanity because we have a large amount of organically generated crap. LLMs could not realistically compete with a curated library of works, and as the online bookstores are flooded with AI generated works and the Waterstones is stocked high with books by authors using a heaping of LLM magic, society will begrudingly accept that “““reading”““ is a question of quality and not quantity.
